# Part 1 — The idea

## 1. FitFirst in one minute

FitFirst is a **shopping helper for independent Indian clothing shops**: saree shops, ethnic-wear showrooms, men's wear, kids' wear and western wear.

A shopper answers a few quick questions: what they're shopping for, their size, what styles they like and (optionally) their skin tone and fit goals. FitFirst then shows them **three to a few garments from that shop's own stock, in their size, that genuinely suit them**. The shopper gets a short code. A salesperson types in the code, sees the same picks, and brings those pieces out.

For the **owner**, FitFirst does something extra. When two pieces suit the shopper about equally well, it quietly gives the **older stock that has been sitting in the shop too long** a small push, so it gets seen. It never pushes something that doesn't suit the shopper.

**In one sentence:** *"We show your older stock to the customers it actually suits, and your salesperson gets a stylist's brain."*

## 2. The problem we're solving

Three people in every clothing shop have a problem.

| Person | Their problem today |
|---|---|
| **Shopper** | Spends 30–60 minutes browsing. Isn't sure what suits them. Often needs family approval. Gets shown things that aren't available in their size. |
| **Salesperson** | Loses time on undecided shoppers. Can't remember every piece in stock, which sizes are left, or where they are. |
| **Owner** | Money is stuck in **old stock** that doesn't sell. They end up giving big discounts, and don't really know *why* some pieces never sell. |

**The big one is old stock.** Industry research (Vector Consulting, Feb 2026) estimates that about **24% of the clothing and footwear on Indian shop shelves is past its best selling window**. That's money the owner has already spent and can't get back unless the piece sells. This is the main reason an owner would pay for FitFirst.

**Why the shopper uses it:** it's quick and it's about *them*, with personal picks that are actually available.
**Why the owner pays for it:** it helps clear old stock and gives them clear numbers on what sold.

## 3. Who uses FitFirst and what each person gets

| Person | What FitFirst gives them |
|---|---|
| **Shopper** | Three strong picks that suit them and are **in stock in their size**, each with a short "why this suits you" line. Later: "see it on me" try-on, and sharing picks with family on WhatsApp. |
| **Salesperson** | A list of shoppers waiting for help, each with their profile, picks, sizes and (later) the rack each piece is on. Credit for sales they help make. |
| **Owner** | Old stock shown to the shoppers it suits. A report in rupees of how much old stock was cleared, a list of what shoppers wanted but the shop didn't have, and how the staff are using the system. |

There's also a fourth group in the shop: **the person who adds new stock** (photos, details, sizes). Making that job fast is critical. If adding stock is slow, the shop will give up on the system.

# Part 2 — How it works, end to end

## 4. A day in a shop with FitFirst

Here is the full flow, in the order things happen.

### Step 1 — New stock comes in
1. A delivery of new garments arrives at the shop.
2. Each garment is added to FitFirst. Today staff type in the details or upload a spreadsheet. The plan is a **"quick add"** that takes about 90 seconds per item: photograph the front, photograph the price tag, scan the barcode.
3. **AI looks at the photo** and suggests details: category (saree, kurta…), colour, pattern, fit. Staff check the suggestions and fix anything marked **"needs review"**. The AI never quietly guesses. If it isn't sure, a person decides.
4. The garment goes **live**, and from then on it can be recommended to shoppers.

For expensive pieces and old stock, the plan also has a **"full add"** with more photos (border, pallu, fabric close-up), taking about 3 minutes per item.

**Getting started at a new shop:** we don't photograph the whole shop on day one. We start with the **300 oldest pieces**, because that's where the owner's money is stuck. Where the shop's billing software can export its item list and sales history, we import it, which saves a lot of typing.

### Step 2 — A shopper walks in
The shopper (or a salesperson helping them) goes through these screens:

1. **Welcome**
2. **Privacy notice:** what we look at, that no photos are stored, and an "I'd rather not" option.
3. **Department:** Men, Women or Kids.
4. **Size:** only sizes that make sense for that department.
5. **Style preferences (optional):** e.g. saree, kurta, floral, embroidered.
6. **Skin tone and fit (optional):** the shopper picks a tone from swatches, or uses a quick camera check that runs on the device itself. Nothing is uploaded and nothing is saved.
7. **Picks:** the garments that suit them, from this shop's stock, in their size.
8. **Handoff code:** a short code (like `7K2Q9X`) to show the salesperson.

If the shopper walks away halfway, the screen warns, then resets itself for the next person. If they want to change an answer, "Edit preferences" updates their picks and keeps the same code.

**Planned improvements to this flow:**
- Choose a language first: English, Hindi or Gujarati.
- Ask category, **occasion** (festive, wedding, office, daily) and **budget** early.
- Ask "Who is this for?": me, someone else, or family.
- Show **3 hero picks first**, then up to 6 more.
- A QR code alongside the handoff code, so picks can be opened on the shopper's phone.

### Step 3 — The salesperson takes over
1. The salesperson opens the staff screen and types in the code (later, they'll see a live queue of waiting shoppers instead).
2. They see the shopper's answers and picks.
3. They bring those pieces out. Later they'll also see which **rack** each piece is on.
4. Later they'll mark each piece as tried, rejected (with a reason) or bought.

### Step 4 — The sale
1. The shopper buys.
2. The sale is recorded **against that code**. That's how we know a recommendation led to a sale.
   - Today staff record it on the staff screen.
   - In future, the code goes into the shop's own bill, or (if the shop uses our billing) the cashier scans the shopper's QR and the link is made automatically.
3. Stock goes down by one, so FitFirst won't recommend a piece that has sold out.

### Step 5 — The owner sees the results
The owner's screen (and later, a WhatsApp daily summary) shows:
- how many shoppers used it
- how many bought something that was recommended
- **old stock cleared, in ₹**
- how much more old stock sold because of FitFirst, compared with a control group (explained in section 8)
- **what shoppers wanted but the shop didn't have**, e.g. *"22 shoppers wanted festive kurtis in XL under ₹2,000 in warm colours; you had 1"*. This becomes a buying list for the owner.
- which staff are using it

## 5. Three ways a shop can use it

The same software can be packaged three ways, depending on the shop.

| Way | What's in the shop | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Assisted** | A salesperson's tablet or phone, plus a small clip-on light | Saree and lehenga shops, where customers sit at a counter and staff unfold pieces for them. The salesperson goes through the questions *with* the shopper. |
| **QR on the shopper's phone** | A printed QR stand; the shopper uses their own phone | Small shops. The cheapest option. Shoppers can even use it at home before visiting. |
| **Kiosk** | A big touchscreen with a camera and a proper light | Larger showrooms. The most accurate skin-tone check and the "wow" factor, but the most expensive hardware. |

What we've built so far is the **kiosk** style. The current plan says the **assisted** way should come first, because it suits how Indian ethnic-wear shops actually sell. (This is one of the questions in Part 7.)

## 6. How FitFirst chooses what to show

This is the heart of the product, explained without the maths.

**Rule 1 — Only real, available pieces.** A garment is considered only if it's active, in the right department, in the **shopper's size**, and in stock. (Planned: also within the shopper's budget, with a small margin, and matching the occasion.)

**Rule 2 — Score how well it suits this shopper.** Each garment gets a "suits you" score built from:
- **colour** against the shopper's skin tone
- **shape and cut** against the shopper's fit goals
- **proportion** (e.g. height against border width)
- **occasion, season and fabric**
- **the shopper's own style preferences**

These rules are fixed and written down. There is no "black box". Any owner or stylist can ask "why was this shown?" and get an answer. The plan is to have the rules written and checked by **one or two professional stylists**.

**Rule 3 — A small, capped push for old stock.** Among pieces that suit the shopper well, older stock gets a small nudge upward. Three guardrails protect the shopper:
- The nudge is **so small that an old piece can never beat a piece that clearly suits the shopper better**.
- **At most 2 of the top 5** picks can be nudged pieces.
- Not every shopper sees the same old saree. The push is spread around.

The owner chooses how strong the push is: **Off, Low, Medium or High**.

**Rule 4 — Honesty.** If a piece is shown partly because the shop is promoting it, it's labelled **"Store pick · offer"**, and a one-line note explains how picks are chosen. We **never show a "92% match"** number, because the score isn't a real percentage and would mislead people.

**Rule 5 — Variety.** The picks shouldn't be five near-identical maroon sarees. FitFirst mixes colours, patterns, cuts and prices.

**Rule 6 — Explain every pick** in one simple line, e.g. *"Deep maroon brings out your warm undertone"* or *"A-line cut balances your hips"*. These lines come from fixed templates, so they're always consistent and polite, and they'll be translated into Hindi and Gujarati.

**The one promise we make to shoppers and owners:** *"We never recommend what doesn't suit you."*

## 7. Skin tone and body: doing it respectfully

This is a sensitive area in India, and getting it wrong is a real risk to the brand and even a legal one.

**Language rules we follow:**
- We **never** use words like "fair", "dark" or "wheatish", and never promise anyone will "look fairer" or "look slimmer".
- Skin tone is shown neutrally, e.g. *"Tone 6 · warm undertone · medium contrast"*.
- The early version of the app used colour-based beauty words for its swatches. That's being replaced with numbered tones.
- All shopper-facing wording should be checked by a stylist and a sensitivity reviewer, in all three languages.

**How skin tone is found:**
- **Option 1 — Swatches plus a short quiz:** the shopper picks the closest swatch and answers three easy questions: *Gold or silver jewellery? Wrist veins green, blue or both? In the sun do you tan, burn or both?*
- **Option 2 — Camera:** a quick check that runs **on the device itself**. No photo leaves the device and nothing is saved. To be reliable it needs good, steady lighting, and the camera version we have today hasn't been tested under real shop lighting. A much better camera version is planned. If the camera isn't confident, it asks the shopper to pick a swatch instead of guessing.
- The camera is always **optional**, and never blocks shopping.

**Body:**
- Instead of labelling someone's body ("pear", "apple"), the plan is to ask about **fit goals**: *"What should your outfit do?"* For example: balance hips, define waist, look taller, streamline the middle, broaden shoulders, or just relaxed and comfortable.
- An optional body-shape quiz is available for shoppers who like it.
- **Height** is asked (petite, average, tall), because it matters a lot for sarees and kurtas.
- For **kids**: age and height only. No camera, no tone, no try-on.
- The camera does **not** guess body shape. It doesn't work reliably, especially with sarees and loose clothes.

# Part 3 — Proving it and selling it

## 8. How we prove it works

An owner won't pay because we *say* it works. We need to **show** it in their shop, with numbers we agree on in advance.

**The pilot:** we run FitFirst free (or nearly free) in **one shop for about 6 weeks**, on part of their stock. Before it starts, we agree in writing on:

1. **The "kill threshold".** These are the minimum results FitFirst must hit, or we accept it isn't working. It's set **before** the pilot starts and **not changed** afterwards, so we can't fool ourselves. The current starting numbers are:
   - **15%** higher average bill value than before FitFirst
   - **30%** of shoppers who use FitFirst buy something it recommended
   - **40%** of shoppers use FitFirst at all

   (These are starting points and still need to be agreed with the pilot shop.)
2. **The control group (the "holdout").** About **1 in 10 shoppers** (10%) get picks **without** the old-stock push. Comparing them with everyone else shows how much extra old stock FitFirst really sold, rather than a guess.
3. **The "before" numbers (the baseline).** What sales looked like before FitFirst. Ideally imported from the shop's billing software (a year of history lets us compare with the same season last year). If the shop has no software, staff log daily totals by hand for about two weeks before launch.
4. **The old-stock measure:** rupees of old stock sold during the pilot.

**Other things we'll watch:** time from walking in to buying, how many pieces were tried per purchase, whether the staff actually use it, and a one-tap shopper rating.

**A firm rule for the demo:** the demo app is full of **made-up numbers**. They must **never** be shown to a shop owner or investor as real results. Every made-up number gets labelled "illustrative" or "DEMO".

## 9. Billing: "works with your billing software, or use ours"

To prove FitFirst led to a sale, we need to know what was sold. That information sits in the shop's billing system (the "POS", or point of sale). There are two paths.

**Path A — Work with the shop's existing billing software.** We connect to what they already use (Tally, Vyapar, Marg, Busy and others) at one of four levels:

| Level | How it works | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| **Manual** | Staff mark sales in FitFirst themselves | None (always the backup) |
| **Excel/CSV** | The shop exports a file from their software each day and uploads it. FitFirst matches the columns. | Low; build this first |
| **Bridge** | A small program on the billing PC sends sales to FitFirst every ~15 minutes | Medium |
| **Cloud link** | Direct connection to modern systems (Zoho, Shopify and others) | Only when a paying shop needs it |

The easy trick that works with **any** billing software: the cashier types the shopper's **FitFirst code into the bill's remarks/notes field**.

**Path B — "FitFirst Billing", our own simple billing module.** For shops that have no software, dislike theirs, or want one system for everything. It would cover:
- scanning barcodes, discounts (bargaining is normal), and never selling above MRP
- GST done correctly (e.g. readymade garments are 5% up to ₹2,500 per piece and 18% above that, based on the price *after* discount), checked by the shop's CA
- cash, UPI QR and card payments
- printed or WhatsApp bills
- returns and **exchanges** (very common in India)
- end-of-day cash count, plus GST and Tally exports for the CA

It must **work even when the internet is down**, because if billing stops, the shop stops.

We would **not** build full accounting. The shop's CA keeps using Tally.

**Which path for which shop:**

| Shop situation | Path |
|---|---|
| Happy with its billing software | A |
| Chain on a big retail system | A only, never replace it |
| Old or disliked software, or wants one system | B |
| No software (hand-written bill book) | B, starting simple |

Path B is a lot of extra work (an estimated 4–6 weeks for one or two developers, plus CA review and printer testing). A simple version (sale + receipt + UPI QR + WhatsApp bill, no GST invoice) is about 1–2 weeks. Which path the pilot shop needs is one of the most important things to find out.

**Questions to ask a shop owner about billing:**
- Which billing software (and version) do you use?
- Do your garments have barcode tags?
- Can you export your items with stock and purchase dates?
- Who enters new stock?
- How many pieces and styles do you carry?
- Is there internet at the counter?
- Could we install a small program on your billing PC?
- Are you happy with your billing software?

## 10. Virtual try-on ("see it on me")

We already have a **separate working prototype** that takes a photo of the shopper and shows them wearing a chosen saree (or other garment), generated by AI. It isn't connected to the main FitFirst app yet.

**How it would work in the product:**
- The photo is always taken on a **phone** (the shopper's, or a salesperson's), because a full-body photo isn't possible from a counter tablet or kiosk.
- It supports different saree drapes: Nivi, **Gujarati seedha pallu**, Bengali, Nauvari.
- It's for **adults (18+) only**, and needs a **separate yes** from the shopper to send their photo to the AI company.
- The photo is deleted within **one day**.
- Every result is labelled **"AI-generated preview"** (Indian rules from 2026 require this).
- Automatic quality checks block bad results, including any that **lighten the shopper's skin** or change their body shape.
- Shoppers can **share to WhatsApp** with the shop's branding, which is free marketing for the shop.

**Cost:** about **₹6–7 per image** (estimate). With 30 shoppers a day trying 3 pieces each, that's roughly ₹18,000 a month, so try-ons would be limited per plan.

**Second use:** "AI catalogue photos", i.e. on-model photos of the shop's stock for their Instagram or website, which could be a separate paid service.

**When:** the current plan is to use try-on as a **demo only** for pitching, and build it properly only once the pilot has shown that the recommendations work. That's a question for you in Part 7.

# Part 4 — Trust, privacy and the law

## 13. Privacy, consent and legal dates

- **No shopper photos are stored.** The skin-tone camera runs on the device, and nothing is uploaded.
- **Try-on** is the only feature that sends a photo anywhere. It needs a separate yes from the shopper, is 18+ only, and the photo is deleted within a day.
- **India's data protection law (DPDP)** applies fully from **13 May 2027**. Under it, the **shop** owns its shoppers' data and FitFirst handles it on the shop's behalf. We'll need:
  - consent for each separate purpose
  - privacy notices in all three languages
  - deleting data when asked
  - a complaints contact
  - a plan for data breaches
  - a one-time review by a qualified lawyer before the camera or try-on is used beyond the pilot shop
- **Kids:** a parent fills in details. No camera, no try-on.
- **AI images** must carry a visible "AI-generated" label (Indian rules in force since 20 Feb 2026).
- **No hidden ads:** promoted pieces are labelled "Store pick · offer".
- **GST bills** must be kept for years (about 6, to be confirmed with a CA) if we do billing, even if a shopper asks us to delete their data.
- **WhatsApp messages or "remember me" profiles** only happen with the shopper's clear permission.

## 14. "What if…?" situations

| Situation | What FitFirst does |
|---|---|
| Shopper doesn't want the camera, wears a hijab, or is uncomfortable | Swatches + quiz instead; shopping is never blocked |
| Bad lighting or heavy makeup | The camera admits it's unsure and asks the shopper to pick a swatch |
| Shopper is wearing a saree or loose clothes | No body guessing anyway; fit goals instead |
| Shopping for someone else, or a gift | "Someone else" path with manual answers |
| A family or couple | Several profiles in one visit; picks can be shared with family |
| A child under 18 | A parent enters details; no camera, no try-on |
| An elderly shopper, or someone who doesn't read much | Assisted mode, icons, large text, Hindi/Gujarati, possibly voice prompts |
| Nothing suits in their size or budget | Clearly labelled near-alternatives (next size, can be altered, slightly over budget); "notify me"; counted as a **demand gap** for the owner |
| Unstitched piece (blouse piece, dress material) | Shown as "stitching required / available" instead of being filtered out by size |
| Piece sells out before staff fetch it | Live stock check, a short hold on the item, and a suggested swap |
| Shopper walks away midway | Screen resets; counted as "abandoned" so we learn where people drop off |
| Shopper asks to delete their data | Erased by code or phone number (except bills the law requires us to keep) |
| All staff are busy | A queue showing waiting time; codes expire |
| Salesperson doesn't trust a pick | They can see *why*, and override it with a reason, which teaches the system |
| **Staff ignore the system (biggest risk)** | Make it *their* tool: sales credited to their name, rewards for selling old stock, faster than their current way, a 10-minute training script |
| Staff keep changing | Each person has their own PIN, which the manager can reset |
| Festival and wedding season | Sell-by dates for festive stock; **no launches or system changes during peak weeks** |
| Owner hides cost prices | Old-stock value worked out from MRP × a ratio the owner sets |
| Internet goes down | Planned: kiosk keeps working from saved data; billing keeps working offline |
| An AI model we rely on is shut down by its maker | Planned: models can be swapped quickly and tested each time. **This has already happened to us once.** |
| AI costs jump | Usage limits per plan, budget alerts and an off switch |

# Part 5 — Where we are today

## 15. What exists today and what doesn't

**Honest summary:** FitFirst today is a **working prototype for one shop**. It runs on a laptop with made-up demo stock. It is **not** live in any real shop, not online, and not ready to sell yet.

**What works today:**
- The shopper screens: department → size → preferences → skin tone and body (or camera) → picks → handoff code. Idle reset and "edit preferences" work.
- The staff screens: PIN login, adding and editing stock (including uploading a spreadsheet), AI suggestions for new garments with "needs review", looking up a shopper by code, recording a sale, a daily sales log, and a results page.
- The picks are based only on real stock in the shopper's size.
- Recording the last unit of a piece twice is safely blocked.
- The whole shopper and staff path was tested in a browser on 25 September 2026, and five issues found were fixed the same day.

**What doesn't exist yet, or isn't good enough:**
- **Stock is one number per garment, not per size.** FitFirst can't tell how many Mediums of a kurta are left, so it could suggest a size that's sold out.
- **"Old stock" isn't real yet.** The system doesn't actually track how long a piece has been in the shop.
- The **camera** hasn't been tested in real shop lighting; today's version is basic.
- **The AI that tags garments** has only been tested once, briefly, with the real AI service.
- Matching is still **simple**: few colour groups, and one set of body rules for all garment types.
- No real sale records: no bills, payments, returns or exchanges.
- No connection to any billing software, and no FitFirst Billing yet.
- Staff share **one PIN**. There are no individual staff logins or roles.
- Works for **one shop only**.
- No Hindi or Gujarati.
- Try-on is a separate prototype.
- Not online. The demo numbers are random.
- The skin-tone swatch labels still use old wording that's due to be replaced.

# Part 8 — Word list

## 19. Glossary

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Aged stock / old stock / dead stock** | Garments that have sat in the shop past their normal selling time (roughly 2–6 months for western wear, 3–9 months for everyday ethnic wear, 5–15 months for occasion wear, in our plan) |
| **Assisted mode** | A salesperson uses a tablet or phone *with* the shopper |
| **Baseline** | The "before FitFirst" sales numbers we compare against |
| **CA** | Chartered Accountant; checks GST and billing |
| **Control group / holdout** | About 1 in 10 shoppers who get picks without the old-stock push, so the push's real effect can be measured |
| **Demand gap** | Something shoppers wanted (e.g. festive kurtis in XL under ₹2,000) that the shop didn't have; a buying hint for the owner |
| **DPDP** | India's Digital Personal Data Protection law; fully applies from 13 May 2027 |
| **Fit goals** | What the shopper wants the outfit to do (e.g. "look taller"), asked instead of labelling their body |
| **Handoff code** | The short code a shopper shows the salesperson to pull up their picks |
| **Kill threshold** | The minimum results agreed **before** the pilot; if they're not met, we accept FitFirst isn't working in that shop |
| **Kiosk** | A large touchscreen in the shop that shoppers use themselves |
| **Path A / Path B** | A = connect to the shop's existing billing software. B = use FitFirst's own simple billing |
| **Pilot** | A trial in one real shop, for about 6 weeks, to prove FitFirst works |
| **POS** | "Point of sale", i.e. the shop's billing system |
| **"Store pick · offer"** | The honest label on any piece shown partly because the shop is promoting it |
| **Swatch** | A colour sample a shopper picks from to show their skin tone |
| **Tagging** | Recording a garment's details (category, colour, pattern, fit); AI suggests, a person confirms |
| **Try-on** | An AI-made picture of the shopper wearing a garment, always labelled "AI-generated preview" |
| **Undertone** | Whether skin leans warm, cool, neutral or olive; affects which colours suit |
